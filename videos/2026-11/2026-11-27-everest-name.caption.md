Surveyors in India logged the peak as "Peak XV." In the 1850s their calculations showed it was the highest on Earth. In 1856, Surveyor General Andrew Waugh said he could find no local name, so he named it after his predecessor, George Everest.

In 1857, Everest himself told the Royal Geographical Society the naming pleased him personally, yet there were objections. As the Society's Proceedings report it, his name was "not pronounceable by a native of India" and "could not be written in either Persian or Hindi."

Yet an 18th-century map had already recorded the mountain's Tibetan name, Qomolangma. In Nepali, it's Sagarmatha.
