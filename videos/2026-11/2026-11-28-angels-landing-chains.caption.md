Angels Landing in Zion National Park got its name in 1916. The National Park Service says Methodist minister Frederick Vining Fisher, on his first visit to Zion Canyon, "allegedly quipped" that only an angel could land there.

In 1926, park crews built the trail up its sandstone spine, chiseling footholds with hand tools. Walter Ruesch supervised the work, and Walter's Wiggles, the switchbacks on the West Rim Trail, are named for him.

Hikers still take the West Rim Trail to Scout Lookout. The last half mile from there has chains that act as handrails along parts of the route, and drop-offs of up to 1,000 feet. Before the permit program, long lines formed to go up and down the chains.

Since April 1, 2022, you need a permit to go past Scout Lookout (the hike up to Scout Lookout needs none). Apply on Recreation.gov in a seasonal lottery or the day-before lottery: $6 per application (non-refundable), plus $3 per person if you get a permit. The park entrance fee is separate.
